## Mandatory legal-to-AssessmentRule gate

Default: **preserve** a source-supported duty. Excluding a provision is the exception and needs a specific reason stated in `analysis`; doubt is recorded, not used to delete. Do not turn a provision heading directly into a repository evidence checklist. Apply these checks before generating any AssessmentRule:

1. **Actor and normative force:** Resolve the actor in this order: the pinned point itself; its pinned ancestor lead-in; provisions supplied in the input citations; and, for a rule about a property or behavior of an AI system or product (design, data, testing, safety, transparency, controls), the party responsible for building it as the source names it (for example developer, provider, organization or individual, or the system itself). Use the actor wording the source uses. If the actor is still ambiguous between roles, keep the duty with the closest source-named actor and note the ambiguity in `analysis`; ambiguity of actor is a legal-review note, not a ground to drop the duty. Passive wording alone (“a system is inspected/supervised”) does not by itself establish a record-keeping duty for a developer, provider or deployer, but it does not erase a separate duty stated elsewhere in the same provision. Never invent an actor from a catalog title.
2. **Separate responsibility from procedure:** Distinguish public-authority inspection/supervision, deployment/operation obligations, externally performed organizational acts and developer design-time obligations. Classify each proposition rather than excluding by keywords. Only a provision whose whole operative content is a public-authority procedure (no duty on a regulated party) yields no development AssessmentRule. A provision that mixes a procedure with a duty keeps the duty.
3. **Cross-references and conditions in other provisions:** A condition, exception or definition that sits in another provision is carried as an applicability condition, qualifier or `unresolvedConditionBindings` entry. A cross-referenced provision that is not supplied is never a reason to drop the duty or to treat it as satisfied or failed; record it as an unresolved condition for Assessment to check.
4. **Do not manufacture documentation duties:** “Supervision by reports, sample inspection or independent assessment” specifies the authorities' supervision methods. It does **not by itself** require a developer to produce three document types, run continuous supervision, keep proof in a repository or build monitoring software. Require a separate pinned legal predicate before asserting a duty to create, submit, retain or produce any report. A repository file neither proves external performance nor does its absence prove noncompliance.
5. **Select the output (in this order):** (a) a duty whose compliance can be investigated from repository artifacts (code, configuration, tests, build or data pipelines, in-repo documentation) gets a LegalRequirement plus a concise AssessmentRule; (b) a real duty that is external or non-repository gets the LegalRequirement with `nonRepositoryDuty=true`, `coverage.state=NON_ASSESSABLE` and a precise `coverage.nonAssessableReason`, with **no AssessmentRule** for that requirement; (c) a mixed duty is split: the repository-verifiable part becomes an AssessmentRule and the remainder a separate NON_ASSESSABLE requirement; (d) return empty arrays only when no duty is stated at all (see above), and explain why in `analysis`. A provision that says what a system or party must, must not or shall ensure is expected to produce output; empty is the exception. NON_ASSESSABLE never means the law is NOT_APPLICABLE or repealed.
6. **Evidence follows law:** Every `criterion`, `investigationGoal`, `requiredEvidence` and `investigationFocus` must trace to a source-supported obligation. A search heuristic is not a new legal duty. Do not invent reports, dashboards, APIs, schedules, retention periods or “at least one document” requirements. Do not duplicate one proposition into redundant criteria. With no AssessmentRule, emit no document search goals or required-evidence checklists.
7. **Trim criteria, not duties:** For each technical criterion ask whether developer-controlled change could address the stated obligation. Use the answer to drop or narrow a criterion or evidence focus; do not use it to delete the LegalRequirement. If a criterion is doubtful, keep it with a narrow focus and the unresolved condition bound, rather than discarding the rule.

This is Preparation methodology only; it cannot override the API schema, approved scope authority or independent legal review.

## INPUT → OUTPUT → PROCESS legal semantic gate before rule creation

Use INPUT, OUTPUT and PROCESS to **describe and scope** each separately supported normative proposition after identifying its addressee, action, modality and source. They shape the rule; they are not pass/fail tests, and an unanswered question never removes a duty. This is an interpretation of a legal duty, **not** a claim that any specific customer's system is in scope or compliant:

- **INPUT (regulated subject and trigger):** Which data, request, system, risk tier, actor, user cohort or circumstance does the provision cover? Take the trigger from the current point, the pinned lead-in or a pinned cross-reference. Preserve AND/OR, exceptions, timing and boundaries. If the trigger depends on customer facts or on a provision that is not supplied, bind it with `unresolvedConditionBindings` as a condition for Assessment; never treat it as true, false or a reason to omit the rule, and never invent customer facts.
- **OUTPUT (required or prohibited result):** What must the system or party generate, return, display, restrict or prevent? Separate software behavior from organizational reports, filings, approvals, evidence artifacts and public-authority inspection methods. Inspection methods are not automatically product outputs or documents a developer must create. If the provision states no software output, describe the behavior or control it does require; do not invent an API, dashboard, log, template or retention period.
- **PROCESS (who does what, when, and how it can be verified):** Which lifecycle step is regulated and which actor performs it: the software, developer/provider, deployer, operator, independent organization or public authority? Classify the step as (i) repository-verifiable design, build, data or test work, (ii) external organizational or operational performance needing out-of-repository evidence, or (iii) government supervision that imposes no customer software obligation. Steps of type (i) become AssessmentRules, type (ii) NON_ASSESSABLE requirements, type (iii) are explained in `analysis`. If the actor or step is only partly clear, keep what the source supports and record the gap for legal review rather than leaving the provision empty.

**Output decision:** Explicit ID/bulk rule selection is a request to prepare those legal sources; it is **not** an applicability verdict. Prepare every duty the pinned text states. If a genuine developer-facing, repository-verifiable predicate exists, create a **concise rule per independent legally required behavior**, never per document, alternative inspection route or evidence source. Each `criterion` inherits its parent requirement; `investigationGoals`, `requiredEvidence` and `investigationFocus` describe verification methods only and must not enlarge legal scope, fabricate conditions or prescribe a product output the pinned text does not require. Mixed provisions keep actors and stages separate without suppressing a real developer branch.

**Final cross-check:** (1) Where is the INPUT trigger pinned, or is it recorded as an unresolved condition? (2) What OUTPUT or controlled behavior is actually required or prohibited? (3) Who owns the PROCESS step and what can a repository prove? (4) Did any doubt delete a duty that should have been kept with a note? Never convert an evidence gap into a duty or a compliance finding. Do not modify selection authorization, source/version pins, API contracts or the separate legal applicability, development scope and compliance decisions.
